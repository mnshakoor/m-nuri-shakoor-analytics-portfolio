CAMEROON + CHAD // 2020 TO AUG 2026

Two Countries, Two Escalation Logics

Cameroon is accumulating coercive incident volume faster than mortality, while Chad is experiencing a smaller but more fragmented and selectively lethal escalation. The portfolio is built around that divergence, not a country ranking.

Scope boundary. The primary comparison uses 34 matched weekly observations: 4 Jan to 23 Aug 2025 and 3 Jan to 29 Aug 2026. The workbook contains weekly aggregated category-geography records, not raw narrative event reports. Population exposure is missing in 2,129 of 7,598 rows and is not imputed or used in the core statistical battery. All real-world events after 29 Aug 2026 post-date this evidence base.

00 // DATASET

What the data can support

Shape

Event stream represented as weekly aggregated category-geography records. The workbook contains 7,598 records representing 24,740 events across 33 ADMIN1 units.

Coverage

All core event, fatality, type, disorder and centroid fields are complete. Population exposure has 2,129 missing rows and remains missing.

Direction

Event burden, fatalities, lethality, civilian targeting, coercive activity, tactical diversity and persistence all rise with risk. No performance-increasing indicator is used.

Spot checks against the workbook were performed on the first record, a mid-header sample, and the last record. Record count was asserted at 7,598 data rows. No imputation was used.
01 // BLUF

The divergence is the finding

FACT. Cameroon expanded faster in incident volume than in fatalities.

Matched-period events rose from 3,134 to 4,152 (32.5%), while fatalities rose from 1,214 to 1,355 (11.6%). Abduction/forced disappearance rose from 802 to 1,245 (55.2%).

FACT. Chad escalated through several different violence mechanisms.

Events rose from 139 to 300 (115.8%). Mob violence rose from 15 to 70 events and from 40 to 157 fatalities. Air/drone strikes appear at 9 events and 23 fatalities in the matched 2026 period.

ASSESSMENT. Fatality totals alone understate operational change.

The strongest signal in Cameroon is a shift in event composition toward abduction and other coercive activity. Chad's pattern is different: concentrated lethality sits alongside broader tactical diversification.

CAVEAT. The dataset does not identify intent or causal mechanism.

It cannot by itself determine whether coding changes, actor fragmentation, criminal opportunism, communal retaliation or deliberate population control explain the shifts.

02 // METHOD

Deterministic analysis before format selection

Techniques: matched-window aggregation; univariate profiling; Pearson correlation matrix; percentile bootstrap confidence intervals with 8,000 resamples and seed 42; three-predictor OLS control; k-means partition with k=4, n_init=50, random_state=42; PCA; and a derived standardized residual.

Primary analytic n: 29 ADMIN1 units with at least one 2026 event. Because n is below 30, every reported coefficient is paired with an interval and cross-sectional associations are not presented as causal.

Derived measure. The coercive composition residual is z(coercive share) minus z(violence-against-civilians share), standardized within the 29 active ADMIN1 units. Positive values indicate unusually high coercive composition relative to the broader civilian-targeting share. The source measures correlate at r=0.496, 95% bootstrap interval 0.005 to 0.834, n=29.

04 // FINDING 02

ADMIN1 statistics support the distinction, but the control is weak

Figure 2 // Civilian-targeting share versus coercive share
Each point is one active ADMIN1 unit, n=29. Cameroon is gold; Chad is blue. Selected units are labelled.
Derived shares. Pearson r and bootstrap interval are reported in the text. No causal interpretation is assigned.
Figure 3 // Multivariate control on lethality
Outcome is log(1 + fatalities per 100 events). Three predictors only, n=29. Points are coefficients and whiskers are percentile bootstrap 95% intervals.
Derived regression. Predictors were selected after correlation inspection. Parametric p-values are printed; colour uses the conservative p<0.05 threshold.

The coercive share and lethality relationship is negative at r=-0.369, p=0.049, bootstrap interval -0.547 to -0.190. In the three-predictor control, the coercive-share coefficient is -0.583, p=0.125, bootstrap interval -1.008 to 0.016. The marginal bivariate pattern does not remain conventionally significant after control, so the evidence is weak rather than decisive.

Reverse-causation path. Lower lethality could permit coercive tactics to persist, rather than coercive tactics causing lower lethality. Aggregated cross-sectional data cannot separate those directions.

06 // DESIGN

Why these figures are built this way

Position carries quantity

Bar length and point position encode the measured differences. Colour identifies country, sign or statistical status.

Uncertainty sits on the figure

Regression coefficients include bootstrap whiskers instead of relying on prose caveats.

The comparison is compositional

The story emphasizes how types of disorder diverge rather than ranking provinces by one score.

Derived values stay labelled

Residuals, shares, PCA coordinates and cluster labels are marked derived wherever they appear.

07 // LIMITS

What would overturn the reading

Establishes

Recorded event composition, lethality, timing and ADMIN1 concentration in the supplied workbook.

Suggests

Cameroon and Chad are deteriorating through different operational mechanisms.

Cannot support

Actor intent, attribution of drone activity, causal effects, or population-level incidence rates without additional denominators and event narratives.

08 // SO WHAT

Different risk models are required

Immediate

Separate coercive access risk from fatality risk in Cameroon. Abduction and movement-control indicators need their own watch layer.

Near term

Treat Chad as a multi-mechanism warning problem. Mob violence, armed clashes, abduction and air activity should not be collapsed into one trend line.

Collection priority

Add actor attribution and event narratives to test the competing explanations. That is the shortest path from descriptive divergence to causal diagnosis.
