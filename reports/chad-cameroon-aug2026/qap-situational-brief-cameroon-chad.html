CAMEROON + CHAD // 2020 TO AUG 2026

Situational Brief, Do not use one national risk model

The same event stream describes two different operational problems. Cameroon requires a coercion-and-access lens alongside battle lethality; Chad requires monitoring for fragmented, selectively lethal escalation across several mechanisms.

Scope boundary. The primary comparison uses 34 matched weekly observations: 4 Jan to 23 Aug 2025 and 3 Jan to 29 Aug 2026. The workbook contains weekly aggregated category-geography records, not raw narrative event reports. Population exposure is missing in 2,129 of 7,598 rows and is not imputed or used in the core statistical battery. All real-world events after 29 Aug 2026 post-date this evidence base.

Different threat architectures

Structural pressure

Cameroon: large, persistent incident volume concentrated in a few conflict regions. Chad: smaller totals but wider mechanism diversity.

Mechanism

Cameroon: abduction and coercive activity expand faster than fatalities. Chad: mob violence, battles, abduction and air activity overlap.

Operational consequence

Access risk and casualty risk should be tracked separately. A single national severity score would hide the divergence.

03 // ACTION

What changes in practice

Cameroon

Add abduction, looting and detention indicators to route and access monitoring. Keep battle lethality as a separate track.

Chad

Escalation warnings should require mechanism-specific triggers for mob violence, armed clashes, abduction and air activity.

Collection

Prioritize actor attribution and narrative event fields in Wadi Fira, Lac, Sud-Ouest, Nord-Ouest and Extreme-Nord.

Decision rule

Do not infer declining risk from flat fatalities when coercive event volume is rising.

04 // CONFIDENCE

Moderate, with explicit flip risks
