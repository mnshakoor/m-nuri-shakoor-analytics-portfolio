West and Central Africa Stability Atlas Eight Switchable Layers Across Twenty-Two States, Including a Bivariate Read of Coercive Fragmentation Against Conflict Intensity

A single page geospatial interface over five composite index families. The atlas is built to make one point structurally: the layer you choose determines the risk picture you see, and the states that look calm on one layer are rarely the states that look calm on the next.

02 // READING
How to Read the Layers Against Each Other

Switching layers is the analytic exercise, not a convenience feature. Three comparisons carry most of the value.

A
Conflict intensity against fragility total
The FSI total layer paints a broad band of severity from Mauritania to the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The conflict intensity layer collapses that band into a much narrower core. Fragility is regional. Violence is concentrated.
B
State legitimacy against conflict intensity
Equatorial Guinea, the Republic of the Congo and Cameroon sit at the severe end of the legitimacy layer and the moderate end of the conflict layer. That divergence is the empirical basis for the central finding in the companion data story.
C
The bivariate layer
The bivariate mode crosses coercive fragmentation with conflict intensity in a three by three key. The diagnostic cells are the off-diagonals: high fragmentation with low recorded violence is the profile that precedes rapid deterioration.
D
The gap layer
The fragility gap layer is the only derived layer on the map. It is included because the states it highlights, notably Equatorial Guinea and Mauritania, are invisible on every conventional risk layer and are precisely the states where acceptance strategies rest on coercive capacity rather than consent.
03 // METHOD
Cartographic and Technical Method
Pipeline: country boundary polygons filtered to the twenty-two study states, reprojected to Web Mercator, fitted to the viewport, and simplified with a distance threshold in screen space rather than in geographic space, so simplification error stays below one pixel at the rendered scale. Paths and centroids are precomputed and embedded. Rendering: vanilla SVG and JavaScript with no external library and no network dependency at run time, so the artefact remains functional offline and archivable. Total attribute payload: under 40 kilobytes.
DecisionChoiceReason
ClassificationContinuous ramp, five labelled break pointsQuantile classing would have manufactured contrast inside a sample that is genuinely severe across the board. A continuous ramp keeps the reader aware that the low end of this region is not a low risk value in absolute terms.
PaletteSequential gold to red for risk layers, diverging blue to red for the gap layerDiverging palettes on a non-diverging variable imply a meaningful midpoint that does not exist. The gap layer is the only variable here with a true zero point, so it is the only diverging ramp.
Missing dataHatched, never imputedThree states lack values on some layers. Imputing them would put fabricated numbers on a map that a reader will treat as measurement.
ProjectionWeb MercatorChosen for shape recognisability at regional scale. Area distortion is modest across this latitude band and no area based inference is drawn from the map.
InteractionHover summary, click to pinThe map answers where. The pinned card answers how much and on what. Separating those two functions prevents the map from carrying quantitative claims that colour cannot support.
04 // LIMITS
What This Map Cannot Tell You
Supported
  • Relative position of each state on each published index for the stated vintage.
  • Visual comparison between layers for the same set of states.
  • Identification of states whose profile changes rank sharply between layers.
Handle With Care
  • The bivariate reading is a descriptive cross-tabulation, not a model output.
  • The fragility gap layer is derived by the analyst and is not published in this form by any source institution.
  • Colour encodes magnitude poorly. Read the pinned card for values.
Not Supported
  • Any sub-national or corridor level inference.
  • Any statement about current conditions. The newest input closes in 2024.
  • Any claim about border alignment or territorial control. Boundaries are cartographic only.
