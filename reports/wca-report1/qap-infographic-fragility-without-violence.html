Key Finding
Across nineteen states, fragmentation of the security apparatus tracks conflict intensity at r = +0.81. State legitimacy does not, and its apparent association collapses entirely once fragmentation is held constant.

Two clusters emerge with an identical mean legitimacy deficit of 8.74 out of 10. One averages 8.14 on conflict intensity. The other averages 4.60. The quiet group also scores worse on anti-corruption policy, 2.60 against 3.29. Governance quality is not what separates them.

MECHANISM
How the Structure Reaches the Field

01 // Structural pressure

Legitimacy deficits at 8.7 out of 10, public service deficits above 8, and socioeconomic development scores near the floor of the BTI scale. These conditions are near universal across the region and they are not what distinguishes violent states from quiet ones.

Present in 19 of 19 sampled states.

02 // Coercive response

Where the state retains a coherent security apparatus, pressure is contained. Where the apparatus fragments into competing formations, auxiliaries and foreign contingents, it converts into open conflict. Fragmentation and external intervention together account for roughly three quarters of the variance in conflict intensity.

R² = 0.760 on two predictors.

03 // Operational consequence

Acceptance negotiated with a central authority holds only while that authority holds a monopoly on force. Once fragmentation begins, every access agreement has to be renegotiated with whoever now controls the road, and the timeline for that shift is political rather than structural.

SO WHAT
Consequence for Security Risk Management

Watch the apparatus, not the government

The leading indicators implied by this analysis are structural features of the security sector: command chain integrity, proliferation of auxiliary and militia formations, foreign contingent presence, and payment or desertion patterns in regular forces. Composite governance scores move too slowly, and in this sample they do not discriminate.

Treat quiet rentier states as deferred, not low, risk

Equatorial Guinea, Mauritania and the Republic of the Congo present as manageable on conventional risk layers. Their calm rests on coercive capacity financed by rents. Contingency planning for hibernation, relocation and evacuation should be maintained at a level proportionate to the structural deficit rather than to the incident count.

Localise the acceptance model

Where the monopoly on force has already fragmented, national level authorisation secures very little. Acceptance has to be built and rebuilt with the actual holders of local control, which is an operational and staffing commitment rather than a document.

Build the panel before the next crisis

A single cross-section cannot distinguish structural drivers from conflict feedback. Three or more index vintages would convert this positional reading into a directional one, and would let the deferred risk states be tested rather than flagged.

ModerateAnalytic assessment and confidence

The association between coercive fragmentation and conflict intensity is strong and survives bootstrap resampling and a two predictor control. Confidence is held at moderate for three reasons. The sample is nineteen observations in one region and one year. The outcome measure is expert coded rather than event based. Causation runs plausibly in both directions, since sustained conflict fragments a security apparatus as readily as a fragmented apparatus permits conflict.

What would change the assessment. A multi-year panel showing that lagged legitimacy predicts change in conflict intensity while lagged fragmentation does not. A global sample in which the fragmentation coefficient collapses outside this region. An event based conflict measure that reverses the association.
