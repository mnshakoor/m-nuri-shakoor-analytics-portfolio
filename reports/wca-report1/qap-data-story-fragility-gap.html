What the Data Is

The dashboard behind this product assembles five independent index families into one country level table. Each family measures something different, and the differences matter more than the overlap.

Twenty-two states appear in the dashboard. Nineteen carry values across all five families and form the analytic sample. Gambia appears in the BTI table only. Cote d'Ivoire and Guinea-Bissau are absent from the FSI panel. Those three states are retained in the dataset and in the map product, and excluded from any statistic that requires the full indicator set. That exclusion is stated wherever it applies rather than silently absorbed.

Direction normalisation. The five families do not share a sign convention. GPI, PPI and FSI increase with risk. BTI and HDI increase with performance, with the single exception of BTI Q13.3. Every derived statistic in this product operates on explicitly signed variables, and no composite score mixes directions without inversion. Getting this wrong is the most common failure mode in multi-index dashboards, and it is invisible in the output.
01 // BLUF
Bottom Line Up Front
01
Fragmentation of the coercive apparatus, not legitimacy deficit, tracks conflict intensity across this sample.

FactFSI C1 security apparatus correlates with BTI conflict intensity at r = +0.81, p below 0.001, 95 percent bootstrap interval +0.62 to +0.94. FSI P1 state legitimacy correlates at r = +0.39, p = 0.10, with a bootstrap interval whose lower bound sits at +0.005. The legitimacy association is marginal on both readings, and in a two predictor regression its coefficient reverses sign and cannot be distinguished from noise.

02
Two clusters share an identical legitimacy deficit and differ by 3.5 points of conflict intensity.

FactThe seven state Contested Monopoly group and the five state Coercively Stable Rentier group both average 8.74 on FSI state legitimacy. Their mean conflict intensity is 8.14 and 4.60 respectively. AssessmentWhat separates them is intact coercive capacity, resource financed patronage, and lower external intervention, not popular consent.

03
Eight states in the sample carry structural deficits larger than their current violence levels imply.

AssessmentEquatorial Guinea, Mauritania, Republic of the Congo, Liberia, Chad, Guinea, Sierra Leone and the Central African Republic sit above the parity line between standardised positive peace deficit and standardised current violence. CaveatThis is a static positional statement about one year of data. It is not a prediction that these states will deteriorate.

04
Mali and Burkina Faso show the opposite signature.

AssessmentBoth record violence well above what their structural indicators alone would place them at, with gap scores of minus 1.45 and minus 1.78. The most parsimonious reading is that conflict in the central Sahel has become partly self-sustaining, driven by armed actor dynamics rather than by the underlying structural profile that produced it.

02 // METHOD
How the Numbers Were Produced
Techniques applied: direction normalisation, z-standardisation, residual gap construction, Pearson correlation with non-parametric bootstrap intervals (8,000 resamples), ordinary least squares with two predictors, k-means partitioning (k = 4, 50 initialisations, fixed seed), and principal component analysis for two dimensional projection. All computation in Python using NumPy, SciPy, pandas and scikit-learn. Quality control: every value extracted from the source dashboard by coordinate parsing rather than manual transcription, then row checked against the rendered table.

The Fragility Gap Construction

The gap is deliberately simple, because a simple statistic survives scrutiny better than a weighted composite whose weights nobody can defend. Both the Global Peace Index and the Positive Peace Index are standardised to zero mean and unit variance within the nineteen country sample. The gap is the standardised positive peace score minus the standardised negative peace score.

A positive gap means the state looks structurally weaker than its current level of violence would suggest. A negative gap means violence is running ahead of structure. The two indices correlate at r = +0.52 within this sample, which is high enough to be meaningful and low enough that the residual carries information.

CaveatStandardising within a nineteen country regional sample means every score is relative to this region, not to the world. A gap of zero here describes a state that is typical for West and Central Africa. It does not describe a stable state.

04 // FINDING 02
Legitimacy Is Not the Variable

A large share of humanitarian and security risk writing on this region, including work I have produced myself, leads with state legitimacy failure as the organising explanation for operational risk. This dataset does not support that ordering. It supports a different one.

Figure 3 // Correlation With BTI Conflict Intensity
Pearson r with 95 percent bootstrap confidence intervals, 8,000 resamples, n = 19. Slate markers indicate coefficients that are not distinguishable from zero at p below 0.05.
Bootstrap intervals are percentile intervals. With n = 19 they are wide by construction, which is exactly why they are shown rather than bare coefficients. Note the disagreement on P1 state legitimacy: the percentile interval clears zero by 0.005 while the parametric test returns p = 0.10. Where two defensible tests disagree at the margin the correct reading is that the evidence is weak, not that one test wins.

Security apparatus fragmentation and external intervention both correlate with conflict intensity above +0.80 with intervals that exclude zero comfortably. State legitimacy sits at +0.39 with a lower bound at zero. Rule of law sits at minus 0.34 with an interval that spans zero. Those two results should change how the causal story is written.

Holding Fragmentation Constant

Correlation alone would leave the question open, because legitimacy and fragmentation are themselves related. A two predictor regression settles it within this sample.

CaveatNineteen observations, one time slice, and two predictors chosen after inspecting correlations. This is a description of structure in one dataset. It is not a causal model, and the external intervention indicator is partly endogenous, since intervention follows conflict as well as accompanying it. The finding is held at moderate confidence and is offered as a reordering of priors, not as a settled result.

Legitimacy deficit is common to both. What varies is whether the state retains a coherent monopoly on force and whether it can finance patronage from rents.

That placement is driven by BTI governance scores drawn from a review window that closes before the July 2023 seizure of power, and by the resulting Democracy regime coding. On present conditions Niger belongs with the Contested Monopoly group. The clustering output is reported as computed and the override is recorded here rather than applied silently to the data.
06 // DESIGN
Why the Visualisations Are Built This Way

Three decisions shaped every figure in this product.

01
Position over colour
Every quantitative claim is encoded as position on a common scale. Colour carries category only. Readers decode position accurately and decode colour intensity poorly, and a choropleth of a composite index invites exactly the misreading this product is arguing against.
02
Uncertainty is drawn, not disclaimed
Figure 3 shows bootstrap intervals rather than bare coefficients, because with nineteen observations the interval is the finding. A bar chart of correlation coefficients alone would have overstated the evidence by a wide margin.
03
The comparison is the argument
Figure 5 exists because the strongest claim in this product is a comparison between two groups on one indicator. Building a figure whose only job is to carry the argument is more useful than a dashboard that shows everything and asserts nothing.
04
Derived values are labelled
The fragility gap and the FSI totals are computed for this product. Neither is published by the source institutions in this form. Every figure note states whether a value is sourced or derived, so a reader can audit the chain without opening the code.
07 // LIMITS
Limitations and Falsifiability
What This Establishes
  • The association between coercive fragmentation and conflict intensity is strong and robust to bootstrap resampling in this sample.
  • State legitimacy carries no independent association with conflict intensity once fragmentation is controlled.
  • Two clusters with identical legitimacy profiles differ sharply in violence output.
  • Positive peace deficit and current violence diverge systematically in eight states.
What It Suggests
  • Operational risk models weighted toward governance quality may misrank rentier autocracies.
  • Coercively stable states carry deferred rather than absent risk.
  • Central Sahel violence has decoupled from its structural baseline.
  • Regime type coding is a weak operational discriminator relative to security sector coherence.
What It Cannot Support
  • Any causal claim. This is one cross-section with nineteen observations.
  • Any forecast of onset, timing or location of violence.
  • Sub-national inference. Every value is a national aggregate.
  • Currency. The most recent input closes in 2024.

08 // SO WHAT
Operational Consequence for Security Risk Management

For an NGO security risk manager, the practical consequence is a change in what gets monitored. If fragmentation of the coercive apparatus is the variable that tracks violence, then the indicators worth watching are the ones that describe that fragmentation directly.

Immediate

Re-weight country risk models away from composite governance scores and toward security sector coherence. Command chain integrity, parallel force structures, auxiliary and militia proliferation, and foreign force presence are the leading terms.

Near term

Treat the eight positive gap states as deferred risk rather than low risk. Acceptance strategies built on the current calm in Equatorial Guinea, the Republic of the Congo or Mauritania rest on coercive capacity that can change faster than any structural indicator will register.

Medium term

Build the panel. A single cross-section cannot separate structural drivers from conflict feedback. Three or more index vintages would allow the lagged tests in F1 and turn a positional finding into a directional one.
