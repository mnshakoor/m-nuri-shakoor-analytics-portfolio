01 // BLUF
Bottom Line Up Front
01
The investigation question came from an anomaly the clustering surfaced, not from a prior hypothesis.

FactTwo clusters returned an identical mean state legitimacy deficit of 8.74 while differing by 3.54 points of conflict intensity. That coincidence was not sought. It was found, then interrogated.

02
Retained coercive capacity is the best supported explanation available within this dataset.

AssessmentThe quiet cluster records lower security apparatus fragmentation (6.06 against 8.87), materially higher BTI stateness (6.30 against 3.86), and lower external intervention (5.18 against 8.20), while scoring worse than the violent cluster on anti-corruption policy (2.60 against 3.29). Governance quality does not separate the two groups. Coercive coherence does.

03
The rent-financed patronage hypothesis is plausible, consistent, and untestable here.

CaveatThe model proposed it and the analyst retained it as a hypothesis while ruling it non-diagnostic. The dataset contains no fiscal, resource revenue or transfer variable. Consistency with an untested mechanism is not evidence for it, and the distinction is recorded rather than blurred.

04
Cameroon is the disconfirming case inside the quiet cluster, and it is reported rather than removed.

FactCameroon carries C1 fragmentation of 8.1 and conflict intensity of 7.0, both far above its cluster mean. The k-means partition placed it there on the strength of development and legitimacy variables. The exception weakens the cluster-level generalisation and is left in the sample.

02 // WORKFLOW
Where the Machine Sits in the Pipeline
Design principle: the model is placed where language and structure are the work, and kept out of everywhere that measurement is the work. Extraction, computation, and validation are deterministic and reproducible. Hypothesis generation, framing, adversarial challenge and drafting are model assisted with a recorded analyst ruling at each gate.
The gate is the point of the diagram. Nothing crosses from the model-assisted layer into the published product without a recorded ruling, and nothing crosses from the model-assisted layer into the deterministic layer at all.

04 // ACH
Analysis of Competing Hypotheses

The anomaly to be explained: two clusters with a mean state legitimacy deficit of 8.74 each, separated by 3.54 points of conflict intensity. The model proposed six candidate explanations. Two were merged and four were carried into a formal ACH matrix scored against evidence available inside the dataset.

IDHypothesisEvidence ForEvidence AgainstDiagnosticityVerdict
H1 Retained coercive capacity suppresses the expression of grievance without resolving it. Fragmentation 6.06 against 8.87. BTI stateness 6.30 against 3.86. External intervention 5.18 against 8.20. Fragmentation correlates with conflict intensity at r = +0.81 across the full sample. Cameroon sits inside the quiet cluster with fragmentation of 8.1 and conflict intensity of 7.0. Gabon and Equatorial Guinea are small states where low absolute violence may reflect scale rather than capacity. High Best supported
H2 Resource rents finance patronage that substitutes for legitimacy. Socioeconomic development 3.00 against 1.14. HDI 0.613 against 0.447. Every member of the quiet cluster is an extractive or hydrocarbon economy. The dataset contains no fiscal, revenue, or transfer variable. HDI and BTI Q6 measure outcomes, not rent capture. The mechanism is inferred entirely from case knowledge outside the data. Low Retained, not tested
H3 The gap is a measurement artefact. BTI conflict intensity records armed conflict, not state repression, so repressive states appear artificially calm. Anti-corruption policy is worse in the quiet cluster, 2.60 against 3.29, which shows the two groups are not separated by governance quality. FSI P3 human rights, an independent repression proxy from a different publisher, is lower in the quiet cluster at 7.04 against 8.17. If repression were being mismeasured as calm, this indicator should move the other way. High Weakened
H4 The quiet is temporary. These states are pre-conflict rather than stable. The quiet cluster records the highest mean fragility gap in the sample at +0.75, meaning structural deficit runs ahead of observed violence in all five members except Cameroon. A single cross-section cannot support a temporal claim. Nothing in this dataset distinguishes deferred conflict from durable coercive stability. Low Untestable here
Diagnosticity note. Diagnosticity here scores how sharply the evidence discriminates between hypotheses, not how plausible a hypothesis feels. H2 and H4 are both plausible and both non-diagnostic within this dataset. Marking them low is the finding about the data, not a judgment on the hypotheses. Testing H2 requires fiscal data. Testing H4 requires a panel.

Pattern. Four of the five errors are silent failures. None of them raised an exception, produced a malformed output, or looked wrong on the page. That is the defining risk of automated and model-assisted analysis: the failure mode is a plausible answer, not a broken one. Every control in the workflow above exists because of that asymmetry.

08 // CONFIDENCE
Confidence and Residual Uncertainty
ModerateOverall confidence in the H1 finding

The statistical relationship between coercive fragmentation and conflict intensity is strong, survives bootstrap resampling, and holds under a two predictor control. Confidence is held at moderate rather than high for three reasons: the sample is nineteen observations in one region and one time slice; the outcome variable is expert coded rather than event based; and the mechanism connecting fragmentation to violence is inferred from association, not observed.

Flip risk 01

Reverse causation. Sustained conflict fragments a security apparatus at least as readily as a fragmented apparatus permits conflict. A cross-section cannot separate the two directions, and the external intervention indicator carries the same problem in sharper form.

Flip risk 02

Coder correlation. FSI C1 and BTI Q13.3 are produced by different institutions, which is what makes the correlation worth reporting. Both nonetheless draw on overlapping public reporting, so a portion of the association may reflect shared inputs rather than a shared underlying reality.

Flip risk 03

Cluster instability. With nineteen observations and four clusters, membership is sensitive to feature selection. Cameroon's placement is the visible symptom. A different but defensible feature set could dissolve the anomaly this investigation was built to explain.
